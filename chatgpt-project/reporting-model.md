# Reporting Model

Portfolio reporting should help leaders make decisions, not narrate activity.

## Reporting layers

| Layer | Audience | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | PPMO and portfolio owners | What exists, who owns it, where it sits |
| Intake | Sponsors and intake forum | What is entering, incomplete, deferred, or ready |
| Portfolio health | Executives and governance council | What is on track, blocked, at risk, or changing |
| Decision view | Executive council | What decisions are required and by whom |
| Follow-through | PMO operations | What decisions, actions, risks, and owners carry forward |

## Required reporting concepts

Initiative count by lane, sponsor, stage, and status; unowned or weakly sponsored work; incomplete intake and evidence gaps; blocked work and blocker owners; decision requests and decision owners; capacity and sequencing concerns if known; material changes since last review; and risks requiring acceptance, mitigation, or escalation.

## Reporting output

Produce reporting purpose statement, audience map, standard views, required fields by view, cadence, source data assumptions, known data gaps, and what each report should cause leaders to decide or inspect.

## Guardrail

Do not invent precision. If the baseline is weak, state that the first reporting goal is portfolio visibility, not performance certainty.
